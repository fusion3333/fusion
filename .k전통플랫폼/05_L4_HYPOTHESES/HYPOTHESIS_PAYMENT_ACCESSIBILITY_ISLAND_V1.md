# L4 HYPOTHESIS — Payment Accessibility Island V1

Date: 2026-08-27
Status: HYPOTHESIS
Parent observations: `03_L2_OBSERVATIONS/MONEY_FLOW_BOOK_PAY_OBSERVATIONS_V1.md`

## Hypothesis
A meaningful subset of Korean traditional-tourism suppliers may have viable assets and demand but remain economically isolated from foreign FIT travelers because the transaction path is incomplete.

Candidate structure:
`High Asset Quality + Observable Demand + Weak Global Discoverability/Booking/Payment/Payout Connectivity → Payment Accessibility Island`

This is NOT yet proven.

## Why this hypothesis exists
Verified platform architecture shows that global OTAs can solve multiple transaction layers for suppliers: traveler checkout, platform collection, supplier payout, distribution and in some cases payment-method localization. Official Korean tourism data also show that foreign card/simple-payment behavior can be observed partially. What remains under-observed is whether traditional-tourism suppliers themselves accept foreign travelers directly and whether OTA participation closes that gap economically.

## Variables required for validation
For each supplier/entity:
- direct foreign-card acceptance (yes/no/conditional)
- wallet acceptance by nationality-relevant method
- English/Japanese/Chinese booking path
- instant booking availability
- OTA/platform listings
- supplier payout method/currency
- commission rate
- settlement delay
- refund/chargeback burden
- capacity
- foreign visitor share
- desire to increase foreign sales
- current price and contribution margin

For demand segment:
- nationality/age/purpose/activity interest
- regional visitation
- spend/WTP proxy
- booking-channel preference
- payment-method preference

## Falsification conditions
Reject or downgrade if:
1. traditional suppliers already have high direct foreign-payment and booking accessibility;
2. OTA listing does not materially increase reachable demand or conversion;
3. platform commission/operating cost destroys supplier margin;
4. suppliers lack capacity or willingness to serve foreign visitors;
5. apparent payment friction is primarily language/discovery/transport rather than transaction.

## Ground-test design
Use a stratified supplier sample across hanok, tea/darye, traditional food, craft, temple, wellness, alcohol and master-artisan experiences.

Compare four groups:
A. direct booking + foreign payment + OTA
B. direct booking + foreign payment, no OTA
C. weak direct payment, OTA present
D. weak direct payment, no OTA

Measure actual foreign share, inquiry-to-booking conversion if available, cancellation/refund pain, net margin and willingness to join a distribution layer.

## Status rule
Remain `HYPOTHESIS` until at least one supplier sample and one traveler/demand dataset are linked. Do not promote to L5 merely because platform architecture makes it plausible.
