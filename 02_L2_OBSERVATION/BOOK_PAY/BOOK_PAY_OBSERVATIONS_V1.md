# BOOK/PAY Observations V1

## O1 — Payment accessibility is stage-specific
A single `foreign_card_acceptance` variable destroys information. The same traveler/card may be usable for online reservation, unusable for prepaid-card top-up, and irrelevant for transit tap.

## O2 — Policy upgrades create time regimes
Intercity bus overseas-card expansion (2024) and Seoul vending-machine international-card expansion (2026) create structural breaks. Historical comparisons must be regime-aware.

## O3 — Accessibility can improve while economic friction remains
International-card support can remove a hard barrier while introducing/retaining service fees, FX/DCC costs or refund friction. Therefore accessibility and transaction cost are separate nodes.

## O4 — BOOK and PAY must be separated
Inventory/terms/account requirements can kill a journey before payment starts. Conversely a successful booking attempt can fail at authentication/authorization.

## O5 — public data is success-path biased
Official sources are strong at describing enabled paths but weak at attempted-but-failed denominators. Failure Journey therefore cannot be inferred from support matrices alone.

## Candidate gaps
- BOOKABILITY_GAP: desired product exists but no usable foreigner booking path.
- IDENTITY_ACCOUNT_GAP: domestic phone/account/identity prerequisite blocks short-term visitor.
- PAYMENT_ACCESS_GAP: booking possible but foreign payment method unavailable.
- AUTHORIZATION_GAP: method nominally supported but issuer/brand/authentication fails.
- FEE_FRICTION: transaction works but fees/FX/DCC reduce conversion/value.
- REFUND_GAP: purchase succeeds but cancellation/refund path is unusable or costly.
- CHANNEL_FRAGMENTATION: different stages require different apps/sites/payment methods.

## Link to opportunity engine
HighDemand + HighQualitySupply + Discoverable + BookabilityGap is a distribution opportunity.
HighBookStarts + HighPayDeclines is a payment integration opportunity.
HighSuccessfulPayment + HighRefundPain is a trust/after-sales opportunity.
These remain candidate patterns until denominators exist.
