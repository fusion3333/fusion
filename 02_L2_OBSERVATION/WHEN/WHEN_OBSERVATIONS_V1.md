# WHEN — L2 Observations

1. WHEN is a temporal conditioning layer, not a month label. The same origin segment can change behavior because its own holiday calendar, Korean events, airfare/capacity, visa regime, FX, weather and shocks change simultaneously.
2. Korea has sufficient official monthly inbound backbone to construct a long panel; the harder problem is comparability and causal attribution.
3. 2015 MERS and 2020-2022 COVID demonstrate why trend/seasonality/shock must be separated. A large YoY change can be base effect rather than structural demand.
4. 2016 official interpretation explicitly links summer growth with vacation timing, short-haul demand and transport supply; therefore calendar demand and access supply must be separate nodes.
5. Potential-visitor timing and actual-arrival timing must be kept as separate measurement families.
6. The engine should compare the same origin×segment across months and years, then mirror the same origin against competitor destinations. This avoids treating Korea-only seasonality as universal outbound seasonality.
7. Lead-lag is critical: content/search shocks may precede booking and arrival by different lags across short-haul and long-haul markets. Public monthly data can detect aggregate lag candidates but not individual conversion paths.
8. Temporal opportunity can occur when demand is displaced rather than absent: high interest + constrained flights/high prices/crowding may move demand into adjacent months or competitor destinations.
9. Therefore future opportunity detection should test shoulder-season gaps, event-driven pulses, origin-holiday mismatches, route-capacity bottlenecks and shock-recovery asymmetry.

No final opportunity score or causal coefficient is assigned at this layer.
