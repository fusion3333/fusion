## Verified observations

1. The official 2024 survey task specification explicitly defines the operational `Middle East` routing frame as: Uzbekistan, Turkey, Israel, Iran, Kazakhstan, Jordan, Saudi Arabia, Iraq, Kyrgyzstan, Syria, Lebanon, United Arab Emirates, Kuwait, Republic of Yemen, Oman, Tajikistan, Bahrain, Palestine, Cyprus, Qatar and Turkmenistan.

2. This frame is not a pure geographic Middle East aggregate. It includes Central Asian states and other transregional states. Therefore the survey label `Middle East` must not be mechanically equated with an external geopolitical Middle East definition.

3. The same official task specification routes the above `Middle East` group through the English questionnaire. English is also assigned to Singapore, Malaysia, Australia, United States, Canada, United Kingdom, India and Other countries. The source describes the survey as using 12 languages and lists English, Japanese, Chinese simplified/traditional, Thai, German, French, Russian, Filipino, Vietnamese, Indonesian and Mongolian. Preserve that official wording rather than independently re-counting language variants.

4. The official request for proposals identifies a concrete composition limitation: the existing survey's `Middle East` respondents contained a substantial share of Central Asians, mostly Kazakhstan respondents, and the contractor was asked to consider increasing actual Middle Eastern respondent representation in consultation with the commissioning body.

## Evidence boundary

The procurement documents verify the intended 2024 operational frame, questionnaire-language routing and a known sampling/composition concern. They do not prove final raw country-code values, realized respondent counts by constituent country, respondent-level questionnaire language, or whether the final fieldwork deviated from the specification.

No percentage for Kazakhstan or Central Asian composition is stated; none is inferred.
