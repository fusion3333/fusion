# Origin TOP15 Observations V2

1. Origin-side outbound propensity and Korea inbound volume are different measurements. A market can have high outbound propensity but low Korea share; this is a potential acquisition gap, not automatically weak demand.
2. Official source strength is asymmetric by country. Australia/UK/Canada provide rich destination/purpose/spend or stay dimensions; Singapore is strong on monthly outbound volume/mode but weaker on destination in the surfaced series; Indonesia is unusually valuable because official outbound statistics combine immigration, digital survey and MPD.
3. A Korea-only inbound ranking cannot explain destination substitution. Origin mirror is therefore a required parent layer for Competitive Destination.
4. Definition risk is first-order: nationality/residence/citizenship and trips/persons/border-crossings cannot be silently merged.
5. Temporal resolution differs by market. The graph must preserve native annual/quarterly/monthly frequency rather than fabricate a universal monthly panel.
6. The most valuable derived future measurement is KoreaShareOfOutbound = Korea-bound comparable trips / total comparable outbound trips, but only where numerator and denominator definitions/time bases are compatible.
7. Another future measurement is KoreaPreferenceResidual: observed Korea share relative to expected share after controlling for distance, air capacity, FX, income, calendar, visa and competitor attractiveness. This is a future model target, not yet an empirical score.
8. Enterprise search/booking data and social signals should be conditioned on official origin-market denominators, otherwise large internet populations can be mistaken for high travel conversion.
9. TOP15 membership must be calculated from the official Korea full-country annual file for the target year; provisional manually assembled lists are not canonical.
10. Origin mirror discovery is now mature enough to feed the next axis: Competitive Destination.
