# PLATFORM Evidence Completion — 2026-08-28

## Scope
PLATFORM is the mediation layer between traveler demand and supplier inventory. It must preserve discovery, ranking, trust, transaction, customer ownership, support and economics as separate observations.

## Verified evidence primitives
- Google Places supports Text Search, Nearby Search, Place Details and Autocomplete. Place Details can expose address, phone, rating and reviews; language/region can be requested. This is a discoverability/place-information sensor, not an impression or booking denominator.
- Viator supplier materials state that suppliers can list products and distribute through Viator and a broad partner network. Viator Accelerate explicitly links increased commission to additional advertising impressions, while also using popularity and review credibility. This is direct evidence that visibility can be endogenous to platform economics.
- Viator has different commercial roles. In affiliate flows Viator can remain merchant of record; in Merchant API flows the partner owns the customer experience and is merchant of record. Therefore platform name alone does not determine customer ownership or money flow.
- Klook merchant materials describe a merchant portal, digital ticketing, distribution/marketing and multilingual customer support. Affiliate reporting exposes page/referral, booking, sales and commission metrics to participating partners; these are platform-private behavioral denominators, not public market totals.
- Airbnb states that experiences can surface in search, planning recommendations, emails, notifications and itineraries. Airbnb currently states a typical 20% host service fee for experiences, deducted from host payout. Fee observations must be timestamped and product-specific because terms can change.

## Measurement rules
Platform listing != platform exposure != click != booking attempt != completed booking.
Review count != customer count.
Rating != objective quality.
Commission != total distribution cost.
Platform GMV != supplier revenue != supplier profit.
Platform market share must not be inferred from public listing counts alone.

## Evidence gap
Public evidence is strong for platform capabilities and selected commercial rules but weak for Korea-specific impressions, ranking exposure, CTR, browse-to-book, cancellation, repeat purchase, supplier dependency and cross-platform attribution. Those require partner dashboards, supplier ground interviews or owned telemetry.
